What "best AI agency" should mean for you

The best AI agency for your business is the one whose work you have heard with your own ears, whose prices you can read before a sales call, and whose contract lets you walk away with your phone number and your customer records.

Search for "top AI agency" and you will find lists. Read who publishes them. Many are written by companies that earn a fee from the firms they rank, or by the agencies themselves. A list cannot know whether your callers want to book a cleaning, report a burst pipe or ask whether a lawyer will take their case, and those differences decide whether an AI phone agent is useful or embarrassing.

So instead of a ranking, this guide gives you a way to judge any AI agency, including ours, in about an hour of calls and questions.

Ten questions to ask before you sign anything

Ask them in writing if you can. Vague answers are an answer.

  1. Can I hear it working right now? Not a recording: a live number you can call at night and on a weekend, and question as hard as your customers would.
  2. Who owns the account the system runs on? If it lives in the agency's account, ask what you get if you leave. If it is in yours, ask who holds the admin login.
  3. Who owns the phone number? If the agency gives you a new number and it becomes the one customers know, find out whether you can take it with you. The FCC says you can generally keep your number when you change providers in the same area, but your contract and the account holder still matter.
  4. Who owns the data? Call recordings, transcripts, contact records, booked appointments. Ask whether you can export them, in what format, and what happens to them after you cancel.
  5. Who actually sets it up? The person on the sales call, a subcontractor, or a template? Ask for the name of the person who will write your agent's instructions.
  6. What will it not do? A good agency has a ready answer: no legal or medical advice, no quoting jobs it has not seen, no connections to your software until they are confirmed. An agency that says "it handles everything" has not thought about your callers.
  7. What happens when it gets something wrong? Who reviews the call logs, how you report a bad answer, and whether corrections are part of the monthly fee.
  8. Is texting working, and is it registered? Business texting from a regular local number in the US requires carrier registration known as A2P 10DLC. Ask whether the agency's texting is registered and approved, or still pending.
  9. Will it make outbound calls, and under what consent? In February 2024 the FCC ruled that AI-generated voices count as "artificial" voices under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, which means calls using them generally require the called party's prior express consent. Ask how consent is collected and recorded before any outbound calling.
  10. What does it cost, all in, and how do I cancel? Monthly fee, setup fee, usage charges, phone number charges, contract length and the notice needed to end it.

Red flags federal regulators have already named

The Federal Trade Commission has spent the last few years putting specific AI sales tactics on the record. A few patterns to recognize:

  • Income promises tied to AI. In September 2024 the FTC announced Operation AI Comply, five actions against companies it said used AI hype to deceive people, including several selling AI-powered online storefront programs with earnings claims. FTC Chair Lina Khan said at the time that using AI tools to trick, mislead or defraud people is illegal.
  • Growth and refund promises to small businesses. In August 2025 the FTC sued a seller of AI agent software, alleging deceptive claims about business growth, earnings potential and promised refunds, and said consumers, many of them small business owners, lost as much as $250,000. A settlement announced in March 2026 bans the company and its owners from marketing business opportunities.
  • "Passive income" automation programs. In February 2024 the owners of an AI e-commerce money-making scheme agreed to a ban on offering business opportunities, after the FTC alleged most clients did not make the promised earnings or recoup what they paid.
  • A wall of glowing reviews. The FTC's rule on consumer reviews, in effect since October 21, 2024, bans fake reviews, including AI-generated ones that claim to come from people who do not exist.

Two different things share the name. An AI agency that sets up an AI receptionist for your business is a service. A course that promises you will earn money by starting your own AI automation agency is a business opportunity, and the FTC's Business Opportunity Rule requires sellers of covered opportunities to give buyers a one-page disclosure document, and an earnings claim statement if they make earnings claims. If you are being sold the second while you wanted the first, stop.

Contracts and pricing: what to get in writing

A trustworthy agency can put all of this on one page before you pay anything:

The price

The monthly fee, any setup fee, what is included, and anything billed by usage such as call minutes or extra phone numbers. Published prices are a good sign; a price that only appears after three calls is worth noting.

The term

How long you are committed, how much notice you must give to cancel, and whether cancellation fees apply.

The exit

What you keep: the phone number, an export of contacts and call records, your website if one was built, and the agent's instructions.

The limits

What the AI will not handle, what is still pending (such as texting approval), and which connections to your software are confirmed versus hoped for.

Is an AI automation agency worth it for you?

Likely worth it if

  • Calls come in while you are on a job, with a patient or after hours
  • Callers mostly ask the same handful of questions
  • You lose track of leads after the first conversation
  • You would rather approve the setup than build it

Probably not yet if

  • You already answer nearly every call yourself
  • Your real problem is that the phone does not ring
  • Every call needs a judgment only you can make
  • No one will read the messages the AI takes

A printable checklist for the sales call

Print this page (your browser's print command works) and tick each line as you go. If an agency cannot answer one, write down what they said instead.

  • I called a live demo line, at least once outside business hours
  • I know whose account the system lives in
  • I know who owns the phone number and whether I can take it with me
  • I can export my contacts, recordings and transcripts
  • I have the name of the person who will set it up
  • I have a written list of what the AI will not do
  • I know how wrong answers get reported and fixed
  • I know whether texting is approved or still pending
  • I know how consent is handled for any outbound calls
  • I have the full price, the term and the cancellation steps in writing
  • No one promised me a revenue figure or a number of new customers
