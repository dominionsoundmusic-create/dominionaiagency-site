What kind of AI automation agency this is

Dominion AI Agency is an AI automation agency for small local businesses: we configure the AI tools, put them on your phone line and website, and manage them month to month.

Some companies in this space sell a login and a tutorial. Some build one chatbot and move on. We work the other way: the founder, Maurice Johnson, sets up each client system himself and stays responsible for it afterward. There is no call center and no large staff, and every system is built on HighLevel rather than on homemade software.

The work covers the places a small business loses customers without noticing: calls nobody picks up, website visitors with a question at 10 p.m., inquiries that never get a follow-up, and happy customers nobody asked for a review.

What a month with the agency looks like

Setup is the start, not the job. Once the system is live, the monthly work is mostly small corrections that add up:

  1. Read the calls. We review call and chat summaries for questions the agent answered poorly or could not answer.
  2. Fix the facts. Wrong or missing answers get corrected and tested again.
  3. Keep it current. Holiday hours, new services, a new staff member who should get messages.
  4. Check the follow-up. Workflows that stalled or emails nobody opened get a look.
  5. Report. You get the summary your package includes, and a call with Maurice if something needs a decision.

Outbound calls and consent

Inbound answering is the core of what we set up. Outbound calling, included from AI Pro, needs more care. In February 2024 the FCC issued a declaratory ruling that calls made with AI-generated voices are "artificial" under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, which means the consent rules for artificial and prerecorded voice calls apply to them. In practice we configure outbound calls for people who asked to hear from you: a callback someone requested, a confirmation for an appointment they booked, a reply to their own inquiry. Cold-calling purchased lists is not something we set up. This is a summary, not legal advice; your attorney can tell you what consent your calls need.

The FTC has made a related point about marketing: claims about what an AI product does must be true and supported. We hold our own pages to that, which is why each one lists what the system does not do.
