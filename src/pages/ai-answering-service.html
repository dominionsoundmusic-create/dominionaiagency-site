What an answering service is for

An AI answering service covers the hours and moments when no one at your business can get to the phone: after closing, on weekends, and when the line is already busy or the call rings out.

That is the job answering services have always done. A business answering phone service picks up in your name, deals with what it can and passes the rest along as a message. The difference here is who is on the line. Instead of an operator reading your script at a call center, a voice agent answers from the facts and rules we configure with you.

It is a narrower job than a full AI receptionist. Some owners only want the gaps covered and would rather answer every call themselves when they can. This page is for them.

Three ways to set up AI answering

An AI answering service for small business usually starts with one of these. Pick the one that matches when you want help; you can change it later.

After hours only

The AI after hours answering service picks up once you close and hands the line back in the morning. Callers at night hear your business name and a real answer to the basics instead of a beep.

Overflow

Your phone rings first. If you do not answer, or you are already on a call, it forwards to the AI. Busy midday stretches stop sending callers to voicemail.

Every call

The AI answers every time and transfers the calls you want to take yourself. This is closer to a full front desk, and it is how our own main line works.

Call forwarding from the number you already have

The AI does not replace your phone number. It answers on a number of its own on the platform, HighLevel's LC Phone or a connected Twilio number, and your current line forwards calls to it. There are two common kinds of forwarding.

  • Forward every callEach call goes straight to the AI. Used for the "every call" setup and for after-hours periods if you switch it on when you lock up.
  • Forward when busy or unansweredOften called conditional forwarding. Your phone rings first, and only the calls you do not pick up, or that arrive while you are on another call, move on.

How you turn it on depends on your carrier. Verizon's own guide, for example, says dialing *71 and the forwarding number sets up No Answer/Busy Transfer for a wireless line, with the phone ringing three or four times first, and *73 turns it off. Other carriers use different codes or a setting in the phone. Business phone systems and landlines have their own menus. We check this with you for your line, and we test it with real calls before relying on it.

Messages you can actually act on

An answering service is only as good as the message it leaves you. A useful one has the caller's name, a number that works, what they want, and how soon they need it. We build the agent to collect those four things before it lets the caller go, and to repeat the number back.

On HighLevel each call is kept in a call log: caller number, time, duration, the summary, the transcript, the recording and any action the agent took, such as a booking. A post-call email sends the summary to the address you choose, so you can work through the evening's calls over coffee the next morning.

Not a voicemail greeting, not a phone tree

Two cheaper tools get confused with answering services. An automated business voicemail greeting plays your recording and saves whatever the caller chooses to say, which only helps if they leave a message and leave it clearly. A business automated phone answering system, the "press 1 for sales" kind, routes calls by keypad but cannot answer a question.

An AI answering service holds a conversation. The caller asks whether you serve their area in plain words, and gets an answer. That is the reason to pay for it. If your callers would be fine with a menu or a voicemail, save the money.
