Missed call text back is a simple idea: when a call goes unanswered, the caller automatically gets a text from your business number, so the conversation does not end at voicemail. We set it up on HighLevel, but on our accounts it is not live yet, and this page explains why.

What missed call text back does

{{ img('phone-counter-rainy-window.jpg', 'Phone lying on a kitchen counter in front of a rainy window', 1200, 675) }}

On HighLevel, missed call text back can be set up two ways. The built-in setting sits in the phone system settings: you write the message, and the ring timeout decides how long a call rings before it counts as missed. The other way is a workflow that starts when an incoming call ends with a busy or no-answer status, then sends the text.

The workflow version is the one we would use. HighLevel notes that the built-in setting sends a text for every missed call, including repeat calls a few minutes apart, and that running the setting and a workflow at the same time can produce duplicate messages. A workflow can wait a short time, check whether the person already got a text, and only then send one.

Done well, auto missed call text back is a courtesy, not a marketing channel: one message, from the number the person called, about the call they made.

Why it is not live here: A2P 10DLC registration

A regular ten-digit local business number is called a long code, and texts sent from software through one count as application-to-person (A2P) traffic. US mobile carriers require that traffic to be registered before they will deliver it. The system is called 10DLC, for 10-digit long code.

Registration runs through The Campaign Registry, the registry the mobile network operators chose to collect brand and campaign information for 10DLC. It has two parts:

The brand

The business sending the texts. Brands do not register with the registry directly; a messaging provider submits them, and the brand's federal tax ID is used to verify that the business is real. An unverified brand cannot register a campaign.

The campaign

The use case: what kind of messages will be sent and why, such as customer care or appointment reminders. Once a campaign is approved it gets an ID, and carriers enable texting for the numbers tied to that ID.

Carriers ask for this because unregistered business texting is how a great deal of spam reaches phones. Our registration is pending. Until it clears, texts from our system would be filtered or blocked, so we do not send them and do not pretend otherwise.

What covers your missed calls today

Missed call text back exists because calls go unanswered. The other fix is to answer more of them, and that part works now.

  • The AI voice agent picks up. Calls you cannot take ring through to an AI receptionist that answers questions from your facts, takes a full message or books a time. A caller who got an answer has nothing to be texted back about.
  • You get the message by email. Each call produces a summary with the caller's name, number and what they wanted, sent to the inbox you choose.
  • A callback list in the CRM. Callers who asked for a call back are saved as contacts with a task, so nothing sits only in a voicemail box. See CRM automation.
  • Follow-up by email. Where the caller gave an email address and agreed to follow-up, the automated sequence runs by email.

How to respond to a missed call by text, by hand

Nothing stops you from replying from your own phone, person to person, today. A few starting points, written to be edited:

  1. The quick acknowledgment. "Hi, this is [name] at [business]. Sorry I missed your call. I'm on a job until about 3. Can I call you then, or is there something I can answer here?"
  2. The after-hours reply. "Thanks for calling [business]. We're closed for the evening. I'll call you back tomorrow morning, or reply with a good time."
  3. The unknown number. "Hi, you called [business] a few minutes ago. How can I help?"
  4. The returning customer. "Hi [first name], I saw your call. Is this about the appointment on [day]? I'm free to talk after 4."
  5. The voicemail they left. "Got your voicemail about [topic]. I can come by [day] or call you at [time]. Which works better?"

Always say who you are, answer the call they made rather than pitching something new, and stop if they ask you to.

The texting rules that will apply once it is live

{{ img('hands-papers-coffee-counter.jpg', 'Hands resting on printed pages at a counter next to a coffee cup and a phone', 1200, 800) }}

Automated business texting comes with consent and opt-out rules. The two places they come from:

  • CTIA's Messaging Principles and Best Practices, the wireless industry guidance carriers apply to business texting, say senders should get the appropriate consent before messaging, use STOP as the standard opt-out word while also honoring plain requests such as end, cancel, unsubscribe or quit, and send one final confirmation after an opt-out with no further messages.
  • The FCC's consent revocation rules let people revoke consent by any reasonable means. The FCC treats a reply of stop, quit, end, revoke, opt out, cancel or unsubscribe as reasonable on its face, and requires the request to be honored within a reasonable time not to exceed ten business days.

So when our texting switches on, every automated text identifies the business and honors STOP. Whether a text back to someone who called you is covered by the consent you have depends on your business and your state, so check your plans with your own attorney. This is a summary, not legal advice.
