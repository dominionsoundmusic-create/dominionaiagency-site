What AI consulting for small business looks like here

AI consulting for small business should end with a short, ranked list you can act on, not a slide deck about the future of work.

Most owners who call us have heard that AI can answer phones, write emails and run marketing, and they want to know which of that applies to a business their size. The SBA's own guidance on AI for small business makes the same point we do: AI is relatively new, so start small, and have a person review what it produces. Its Office of Advocacy reported in September 2025, using Census Bureau survey data, that AI use among small firms (fewer than 250 employees) rose from 6.3 percent to 8.8 percent over six months. In other words, most small businesses are still deciding, and there is no penalty for deciding carefully.

As an AI consultant for small business, our job is to find the one or two changes that would actually make a difference this quarter, explain what they would cost, and say plainly where AI would be the wrong tool.

The questions we ask first

The first conversation is mostly questions. These are the ones that shape the recommendation:

  • How do customers reach you today: phone, website, walk-ins, referrals, ads?
  • When do calls come in that nobody answers, and what happens to them?
  • What do callers ask most often, and could someone new answer it from a sheet?
  • Which tasks do you or your staff repeat every day or every week?
  • Where do leads or appointments fall through the cracks?
  • What software do you already use for booking, billing or jobs?
  • What information do customers share with you that must stay private?
  • Which questions must never be answered by anyone but you or a licensed person?
  • What would a mistake cost: an apology, a refund, a lost client, a compliance problem?

How we decide what to automate first: a scoring checklist

Each candidate task gets a score from 0 to 2 on six questions. Higher totals go first. A 0 on the last two questions can take a task off the list no matter how high it scores elsewhere.

Risks to weigh before you add AI

A good AI consultant spends as much time on risk as on features. These are the ones that come up for almost every small business.

Privacy and data

Callers and chat visitors tell an AI system things: names, addresses, sometimes health or legal details. Decide what it should collect, what it should never ask for, and who can see the records. A May 2026 GAO report on AI in SBA programs named data privacy and security among the main risks of AI use. Nothing we set up is a HIPAA compliance program.

Accuracy

AI can sound confident and still be wrong. The same GAO report listed inaccurate outputs and over-reliance on AI as risks and said human review can reduce them. That is why our agents answer from facts you approve, hand off what they do not know, and get reviewed after real calls.

What you claim about it

If you advertise that your business uses AI, the claim has to be true. The FTC has warned businesses to keep their AI claims in check, and its 2024 enforcement sweep, Operation AI Comply, made clear there is no AI exemption from the laws against deceptive advertising.

A way to think it through

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework is voluntary guidance built around four functions: govern, map, measure and manage. A small business does not need the full framework, but the habit fits: decide who is responsible, know where AI is used, check how it performs, and fix what goes wrong.

Why the consultant also builds

Plenty of AI consultant services stop at a recommendation and leave you to find someone to carry it out.
