Leads do not wait for you to finish the showing

AI for real estate agents is most useful at the exact moment you cannot be on the phone: in a showing, at a closing table, or driving between listings when a buyer calls the number on your sign.

Buyers still rely on agents. The National Association of REALTORS 2025 Profile of Home Buyers and Sellers reports that 88% of buyers purchased their home through a real estate agent or broker. That puts a lot of weight on how those buyers first reach you, and much of it happens by phone, from yard signs, listing sites, open house flyers and referrals.

We set up an AI voice agent for real estate that answers your line, talks with the caller about the listing they called about, finds out what they need, and gets the lead to you with notes. It is built on HighLevel, and it works only from the listing facts and rules you approve.

Where real estate calls come from

Each source arrives with a different caller and a different question, so the agent opens each conversation a little differently.

Yard sign calls

Often someone parked out front. They want the price, whether it is still available and whether they can see it today.

Listing site inquiries

Callers who saw the photos online and want details the listing does not show, or a time to tour.

Open house follow-ups

Visitors calling back with questions after the weekend, or asking about a second look.

Seller inquiries

Homeowners asking what their house might sell for or how you work. The agent books a call with you; it does not estimate a value.

Referrals and past clients

People who already know you. The agent takes a message and flags it so you call back personally.

Rental and tenant calls

For agents and property managers who handle rentals: availability, showings and maintenance messages.

Fair Housing guardrails written into the script

The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development describes the Fair Housing Act as prohibiting discrimination in housing on the basis of race, color, national origin, religion, sex, familial status and disability. HUD's regulations treat steering, meaning limiting a person's housing choices through words or conduct because of a protected characteristic, as unlawful. Many states and cities add more protected classes of their own.

An AI agent that improvises about neighborhoods could say something no licensed agent would. So we keep it narrow on purpose:

  • It talks about the property: price, size, features, status, showing times and open houses
  • It does not describe who lives in an area, or suggest areas based on who the caller is
  • Questions about schools, safety, places of worship or "the kind of neighbors" are passed to you, so you answer them the way your brokerage requires
  • It asks every caller the same intake questions, in the same order

We describe the law here for context only. It is not legal advice, and your broker and attorney decide what your agent may say.

Outbound calls and the consent rules

Speed matters with new leads, and outbound calling is part of our AI Pro package. It is also the area with the most legal exposure. In a Declaratory Ruling released February 8, 2024, the Federal Communications Commission confirmed that the Telephone Consumer Protection Act's restrictions on artificial or prerecorded voices cover AI technologies that generate human voices, so calls using them need the called party's prior express consent unless an exemption applies. The FTC's Do Not Call rules for sales calls apply as well, including honoring a person's specific request not to be called.

  • We set outbound calls only for people who asked to hear from you, such as a website form with consent wording your broker approved
  • We do not load purchased lists, expired listings or cold-call numbers
  • A caller who says "do not call me" is marked so the system stops

Showing requests and your calendar

HighLevel's voice agent can offer open times and confirm a booking during a live call, using the calendar settings you control, which decide which times can actually be booked. For agents, that usually means a showing request block rather than a hard booking, because access, the seller's schedule and the lockbox are your call.

The agent never gives out lockbox codes, gate codes or door instructions, and never confirms that a home is vacant. It also asks whether the caller has signed with another agent, so you are not surprised at the door.

If you already use a real estate CRM, we check whether leads can be sent into it before we promise that. Otherwise they land in a HighLevel pipeline with the call summary attached. See AI appointment booking and CRM automation.

An AI receptionist for property management

Property managers get different calls: prospects asking what is available, current tenants with a broken dishwasher, and the occasional real emergency at night. The agent is set up with your rules for each.

  • ProspectsUnit availability, rent you approved, pet rules as you wrote them, and showing requests.
  • MaintenanceUnit, problem, access permission and callback number, sent to the person you name.
  • EmergenciesThe situations you define go to your on-call number by transfer. Anyone describing fire, a gas smell or danger is told to call 911.

Fair Housing applies to rentals as much as sales, so the same intake rule holds: same questions for everyone, and no screening decisions by the AI.
