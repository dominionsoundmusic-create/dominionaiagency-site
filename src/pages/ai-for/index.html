AI for small business is not one product. It is a handful of tools, mainly a phone agent, a website chat and voice widget, and automated follow-up, that do very different work depending on who picks up the call.

A family law office needs a careful intake that never strays into advice. A dental office needs a second pair of hands at lunch. An HVAC company needs something to answer the phone during the first heat wave of summer while every tech is on a roof. We build each one on HighLevel, configure it with that business's facts, and write its limits for that trade. Where small businesses are with AI right now

The U.S. Census Bureau asks businesses about AI in its Business Trends and Outlook Survey. In data from December 2025 to May 2026, overall AI use hovered between 17% and 20% of businesses, and fewer than 20% of firms with four or fewer employees reported using AI. The Census Bureau also notes that it changed the question in November 2025 to ask about AI in any business function, which counts simple uses like drafting emails.

In other words, most small business owners are not behind a crowd of competitors. They are deciding where AI belongs, if anywhere, and that is the right question to start with. The Small Business Administration launched an AI for Small Businesses Resource Hub in December 2024 for owners who want to read up on their own first.

How to use AI for small business: practical first steps

  1. Count the leaks for one week. Missed calls, voicemails nobody returned, web forms answered the next day, quotes never followed up. Write them down instead of guessing.
  2. Pick one job. The leak that costs you most. For many local businesses it is the phone; for others it is follow-up. Do not start with five tools.
  3. Write the facts down. Hours, services, the prices you are willing to quote, the questions customers ask, and the things you never want said for you. An AI is only as good as this sheet.
  4. Hear it before you buy it. Call a working example. Ours is our main line. Ask it hard questions, interrupt it, and see how it handles what it does not know.
  5. Read its work. For the first weeks, read every call summary. Fix the answers it gets wrong. That is part of what we do each month.
  6. Add the next job only when the first works. Booking, then follow-up email, then review requests. Each one builds on the customer records the last one created.

What AI does not fix

Being clear about this saves owners money. These problems need a person, a process or a price change, not software.

A process nobody follows

If leads already sit in an inbox for days, an AI that captures more leads fills the inbox faster. Someone still has to call them back.

Prices or service problems

An AI can answer politely that a job costs what it costs. It cannot make a high price feel lower or repair a bad reputation.

Judgment calls

Legal advice, medical questions, diagnosing equipment, pricing a job sight unseen. We configure agents to hand those off every time.

Be wary of anyone who says otherwise. The Federal Trade Commission has told businesses to keep their AI claims in check and not to exaggerate what a product can do, and the same test is useful for buyers: ask exactly what the tool does, then ask to hear it work.

Choosing the best AI for small business owners

"Best" depends on the job, but the questions that separate a useful setup from a costly one are the same in every industry:

  • Does it answer only from facts you approved, and can you see and change those facts?
  • What does it say when it does not know? Ask for that script.
  • Can you read a summary of every conversation?
  • Does it connect to the booking or field service software you already use, or only take requests? Get the answer in writing for your system.
  • Is the price on the page, and what is included?
  • Who fixes it when your hours or services change?

Outbound AI calling needs extra care: the FCC ruled in February 2024 that AI-generated voices count as artificial voices under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, so consent rules apply. We start every business on inbound calls, where the customer chose to call you.
