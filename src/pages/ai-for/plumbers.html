Plumbing calls come in at the worst possible times

An AI receptionist for plumbers picks up when you are under a house, on another job or asleep, and gets each caller to the right next step: a booked visit, a message, or a live transfer to whoever is on call tonight.

Irregular hours are built into the trade. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics notes that most plumbers work full time, including nights and weekends, and are often on call to handle emergencies. That means the phone rings when the office is closed, and the person who answers is often the same plumber who just finished a twelve-hour day.

Plumbing callers are also usually stressed. Someone standing in an inch of water by the dishwasher wants to hear a calm voice ask sensible questions, not a voicemail greeting. We set up an AI voice agent on HighLevel that does exactly that: it asks your questions in your order, writes down the answers, and follows your rules about who gets woken up.

A night call, step by step

Here is the shape of a typical after-hours call once your rules are loaded. It is a generic example, not a recording, and every line in it is something you would sign off on first.

  1. The agent answers with your company name. It says it is the after-hours line and asks how it can help.
  2. Is water moving right now? This is the question that splits the night. Water still running from a pipe, a water heater or a supply line is handled differently from a slow drain that can wait until morning.
  3. Can they reach the shutoff? If you approved it, the agent asks whether the caller knows where the main shutoff is and reminds them to close it. If you did not, it skips this step.
  4. Where is the problem? Kitchen, bathroom, water heater, outside line, sewer backup. The answer goes in the notes so the plumber knows what to load.
  5. Address, access and callback number. Gate codes, pets, whether the caller is the owner or a tenant, and the best number to reach them.
  6. Route it. If the call meets your emergency rules, the agent transfers it to your on-call number. If not, it books the first morning opening or takes a message, and tells the caller what to expect in the words you chose.

Nothing in that flow requires the agent to know plumbing. It requires your judgment, written down once, which the agent then applies to every caller the same way.

What the agent says, and what it is not allowed to say

Lines you can approve

  • Your service area, hours and after-hours policy, including any after-hours fee you want stated
  • Whether you work on tankless water heaters, well systems, sewer lines or commercial buildings
  • A reminder to close the main shutoff, if that is your preference
  • What happens next and roughly when to expect a callback, in your words, without a promised arrival time
  • A flat service-call or drain price, but only if you publish one

The gas rule matters for plumbers who install or repair gas water heaters and lines. Federal pipeline safety guidance from PHMSA tells people who suspect a gas leak to leave the area and call 911 from a neighbor's house or another location well away from the leak, and to avoid using phones or switches near it. So the agent does not ask follow-up questions about the smell. It reads the safety line, ends the call, and alerts you.

The calls that are not emergencies, and why they still count

Most plumbing calls are not floods. They are a water heater that takes forever to recover, a toilet that runs all night, a slow shower drain, a faucet that drips, a homeowner who wants a sewer camera inspection before buying a house. These are the calls that fill the schedule on a normal Tuesday, and they are easy to lose when nobody answers.

Small leaks add up. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency's WaterSense program says household leaks can waste nearly 1 trillion gallons of water annually nationwide. A homeowner who finally calls about a running toilet or a dripping supply line is ready to book, and the agent can put them on your calendar on the first ring.

For these calls the agent books straight into the time slots you set aside, collects the details you need (water heater fuel type and age, which drain, whether it is a rental), and sends you the summary.
