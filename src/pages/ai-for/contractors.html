The phone rings while your hands are full

For most small contractors, AI for contractors starts with one job: an AI receptionist that answers estimate requests while you are working, so the call becomes a booked site visit instead of a voicemail you return at dark.

Homeowners shopping for a project rarely call just one company. The Federal Trade Commission's advice on avoiding home improvement scams tells homeowners to get written estimates from several firms. That is good advice for them, and it means a caller who reaches your voicemail can simply dial the next contractor on the list.

We set up an AI voice agent on HighLevel that picks up those calls, asks the questions you ask on a first call, and puts the estimate visit on your calendar. You still do the selling, the measuring and the pricing. The agent just makes sure the conversation starts.

What a good estimate call captures

You decide which of these the agent asks and in what order. The answers arrive by email and in your CRM, so you can decide from the truck whether the drive is worth it.

  • The jobRoof repair or replacement, kitchen or bath remodel, addition, deck, panel upgrade, new circuit, whatever your trade covers, and whether it is residential or commercial.
  • The scope, roughlyHow many rooms, how many squares, how old the roof is, whether the house has had work done before. Rough answers are fine; you will measure it yourself.
  • TimelineIs this urgent, this season, or next year? Is there an insurance claim, a closing date or an inspection driving it?
  • Budget range, if you want itSome contractors want the question asked, others find it puts callers off. The agent follows your choice.
  • Address and accessWhere the job is, who will be there, gate codes, and whether the caller owns the property.
  • PhotosThe agent can ask the caller to email photos of the area, so you see it before you drive out.
  • How they found youUseful for knowing which ads, referrals or signs are working.

Why the agent never prices a job sight unseen

A number given over the phone before anyone has looked at the job becomes the number the customer remembers. If the decking is rotten, the wiring is aluminum or the wall you planned to move is load bearing, the real price will be different, and you start the relationship explaining why.

The same FTC guidance tells homeowners to compare written estimates and ask why prices differ, which is the conversation you want to have in person, after you have seen the work. So the agent books the visit and, when a caller pushes for a price, gives the answer you wrote: that the price comes after the estimate visit, or a published starting price if you choose to share one.

That is also why AI for general contractors works best as a front desk, not an estimator. It organizes the request; you price it.

Storm season for roofers: when every phone in town rings at once

After hail or high wind, roofing calls arrive in a wave. The FTC's advice on avoiding scams after weather emergencies tells homeowners to ask for IDs, licenses and proof of insurance before paying anyone, and its home repair scam advice lists a contractor who knocks on the door because they are "in the area" as a warning sign. A roofing company that answers its phone, takes the details calmly and books inspections in order is the opposite of what homeowners are being warned about.

Roofing is seasonal in other ways too. The Bureau of Labor Statistics notes that in northern states roofing work may be limited during the winter months, and that roofers may work overtime during the busy summer months. On those long days, the phone is the first thing to fall behind.

  1. Calls picked up while you are on the roof. The agent answers when the office line would otherwise ring out, and every caller is asked the same storm questions.
  2. Inspections booked into the slots you open. You decide how many a day; the agent fills them and takes a waiting list when they run out.
  3. Honest expectations. The agent tells callers what you want said about your schedule, without promising a date you cannot keep.
  4. No pressure. It does not push anyone to sign or to file a claim. It collects details and books.
