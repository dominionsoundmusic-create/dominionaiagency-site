A dental AI receptionist earns its place on the calls that land while the front desk is checking out one patient, confirming tomorrow's schedule and answering a second line at the same time.

Those are the calls that go to voicemail: the new patient choosing between two offices, the parent asking whether you see children, the lunch-hour reschedule. We set up an AI receptionist for dental offices that answers them with your office's name and your facts, collects what your front desk needs, and hands everything else to the people who know the patient. The front desk stays in charge. The agent covers the gaps.

The calls a dental front desk juggles

Not every call is the same job. Sorting them is the first thing we do with an office, because it decides what the AI is allowed to handle.

New patients

Who you are, where you are, whether you take their plan, whether there is an opening soon. Usually the most valuable call of the day and the least predictable.

Existing patients

Reschedules, cancellations, "what time is my appointment," directions. Routine, frequent, and easy for an agent to collect accurately.

Insurance and billing

"Do you take my plan?" is general. "What will I owe?" is specific to the patient and needs your staff and your records.

Pain and injuries

A broken tooth on Saturday, a child who fell. These follow the dentist's own instructions, never the AI's judgment.

Clinical questions

Medications, post-op questions, whether something is normal. These go to the dentist or hygienist as a message, with no answer from the agent.

Everyone else

Vendors, labs, other offices, sales calls. The agent takes a message or screens them out, as you prefer.

HIPAA, business associates and what we keep out of the agent

This is general information from HHS, not legal or compliance advice. Your office's compliance help should make these calls with you.

What HHS says about business associates

HHS describes a business associate as a person or entity that performs functions or provides services for a covered entity that involve using or disclosing protected health information. HHS says covered entities generally need a written contract, the business associate agreement, that requires the business associate to safeguard that information, and a subcontractor handling it for a business associate is a business associate too. HHS publishes sample agreement provisions.

How we set the agent up because of it

We are not a HIPAA compliance program, and this service does not make your office compliant. We keep the agent to scheduling and general questions so it collects as little as possible: a name, a callback number, a reason for the call in a few words. It does not take clinical history, look up charts or read records back. Before go-live we go through with you which accounts and agreements are in place, rather than assume.

HHS also says providers may leave phone messages for patients and suggests keeping them minimal, such as a name and number and what is needed to confirm an appointment. We write reminder and callback wording to that same standard.

New patients: what the AI dental receptionist asks

A new-patient call is short when it goes well. The agent is set up to collect what your office actually uses, in the order you prefer, and nothing more:

  • Name, phone and email, and the best time to call back
  • Whether the visit is for an adult or a child, if your office cares
  • Insurance plan name, or that they will be paying themselves
  • A reason for the visit in the caller's own words: a cleaning, a checkup, a specific concern
  • Preferred days and times
  • How they heard about you, if you track it

Your front desk gets an email with those details and, if the office allows direct booking, the time that was reserved.

Recall, reminders and the lunch hour

Hygiene recall is a list somebody has to work, and it slips when the front desk is busy. The interval for each patient belongs to the dentist: a Cochrane review of the research found little to no difference for adults between six-month checkups and checkups set by the dentist's assessment of each patient's risk, in tooth decay, gum disease or quality of life. So the agent never decides when someone is due. It works from the due dates your office gives it.

What it can do today is answer inbound recall calls, take the appointment request, and send email reminders and follow-ups from the CRM. Outbound recall calls by voice are available on AI Pro, for patients your office has permission to call.

Overflow and after-hours, not a replacement

Most offices start with the agent answering only when the front desk does not pick up within a few rings, plus every call after closing. That keeps the familiar voice on routine calls and gives the AI the ones that would have gone to voicemail. You can widen or narrow that at any time.

Connecting to your practice management software

This is the question every office asks, and the honest answer is that it depends on the software, the version and sometimes the plan. We check what your system allows before we quote anything. There are three outcomes:

  1. Direct booking. The software allows an outside connection for scheduling, and the agent books into real openings.
  2. A synced calendar. The agent books into a HighLevel calendar that mirrors the hours you release, and your front desk moves each booking into the system of record.
  3. Requests only. The agent collects the request and preferred times, and the front desk calls back to confirm. Simple, and it still catches the call.
