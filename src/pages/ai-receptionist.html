What an AI receptionist actually is

An AI receptionist is a voice agent that picks up your business calls, talks with the caller in plain spoken English, and handles the routine part of the conversation the way a front desk person would.

People also call it an artificial intelligence receptionist, a virtual AI receptionist or a voice AI receptionist. The names describe the same thing: a program that hears the caller, decides what the caller is asking for, and answers out loud. What separates a useful one from a frustrating one is not the voice. It is what the agent has been told about your business and what it is allowed to do next.

We set these up for small businesses on HighLevel, and we manage them afterward. You do not get a login and a manual. You get a front desk AI receptionist that has been fed your real hours, your real service list and your rules about who gets which call.

Six jobs it can take off your plate

Each of these is switched on, shaped or left off depending on your business. None of them is automatic until we have configured and tested it with you.

  • GreetingIt answers with your business name and a greeting you approve, at any hour, including the calls that ring while you are on a ladder or with a customer.
  • Answering questionsHours, location, which services you offer, whether you serve a certain town, what to bring to an appointment. It answers from the facts you hand us, not from guesses.
  • Taking messagesWhen a caller needs you personally, it collects the name, the callback number and the reason for the call, and the details reach you after the call ends.
  • BookingIt can offer open times from a calendar you control and put the appointment on it, following the booking rules you set.
  • TransferringWhen a call should go to a person, it can transfer the caller to the number you pick.
  • After-hours coverageNights, weekends and holidays are covered by the same agent, with different instructions if you want them, such as offering the next open appointment instead of a same-day callback.

One call, start to finish

This is a generic example, not a recording. It shows the order of events on a typical call to a small service business after closing time.

  1. The phone rings and nobody is free. Your number forwards the call to the AI receptionist, so the caller hears a greeting instead of a voicemail beep.
  2. The caller asks a question. Say they want to know whether you are open Saturday. The agent answers from the hours you gave it, including the holiday change you sent last week.
  3. The caller wants an appointment. The agent checks the calendar, offers times that are actually open, and books the one the caller picks.
  4. The caller asks something it was not given. A price for a job nobody has seen, for example. The agent says it cannot quote that, offers to take a message, and collects a callback number.
  5. The call ends and you get the record. A summary and the details land in your email, and the call log keeps the transcript and recording so you can check exactly what was said.

How we build it on HighLevel

HighLevel calls these Voice AI agents. Its help center lays out what goes into one, and our setup follows the same pieces.

Approved business information first. HighLevel's setup guide asks for approved business information for the agent's instructions or its knowledge base before you build. That is the part we spend the most time on with you, because an AI receptionist is only as accurate as what it is given.

A phone number on the platform. The agent answers on a number from HighLevel's phone system (LC Phone) or a connected Twilio number, and your existing line forwards to it.

Working hours and booking hours are separate. HighLevel treats the hours the agent answers calls and the times a calendar will accept bookings as two different settings. That matters: the agent can answer at 2 a.m. without offering a 2 a.m. appointment.

A record of every call. Each entry in the call log can include the caller's number, the summary, the transcript, the recording and the actions the agent took, and a post-call email can send the summary to you.

Outbound calls follow different rules

Everything on this page is about answering calls that come in. Calling people is another matter. In February 2024 the FCC ruled that calls made with AI-generated voices count as "artificial" under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, which means the consent rules for robocalls apply to them. If you want an agent to place calls, talk with your own attorney about consent first.
