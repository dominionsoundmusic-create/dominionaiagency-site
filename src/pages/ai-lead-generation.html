What an AI marketing agency should do for a local business

Most small businesses do not need an AI marketing agency to find them thousands of new strangers. They need the inquiries they already get answered, followed up and turned into reviews.

That is the order we work in. First, the leads that already arrive by phone, website and the ads or listings you already run get caught and followed up. Then every finished job gets a review request. Then the social pages and the website get kept current so the next person who looks you up finds an active business.

AI does part of that work: it answers questions in chat and on the phone, drafts posts and page copy for a person to check, and summarizes conversations. Rules in the CRM do the rest. What AI cannot do is make anyone pay attention. Search engines, social platforms and your customers decide that, and we say so on every page.

AI lead generation, described conservatively

"Lead generation" often means buying ads or contact lists. Ours means something narrower and more honest: making sure the people who already reach out do not slip away.

A website visitor asks the chat widget whether you serve their area and leaves a name and email. A caller reaches the AI voice agent after hours and asks for an estimate. Someone fills out a form from an ad you are already running. Each becomes a contact in your CRM with the conversation attached, gets an email acknowledgment, and lands in the pipeline where you or the follow-up sequence takes it from there.

We do not buy contact lists, scrape directories or send cold email on your behalf. Those tactics can get a domain blocked, and commercial email has to follow the FTC's CAN-SPAM rules either way. If you want to grow ad spend, that is a decision for you and whoever manages your ads; our part is making sure the leads it brings are answered.

Reviews, done by the rules

Reviews are the marketing that strangers trust, which is why regulators watch how businesses get them. The FTC's final rule on consumer reviews, announced in August 2024, bans fake reviews, including AI-generated ones, and bars offering a reward on the condition that a review be positive. Google's Maps content policy does not allow incentives such as payment, discounts or free services in exchange for a review.

So the review management we set up is plain: one request, sent to every customer after the job, with a direct link to your review page. No rewards, no filtering out unhappy customers, no reviews written for you. We set the timing and the wording with you, and the request goes out by email today.

More of how review requests fit into the follow-up is on the CRM automation page.

Social media automation without losing your voice

Social pages go quiet for one reason: posting takes time nobody has. Social media automation fixes the time part. AI helps draft a batch of posts from your services, seasons and photos, you approve or change them, and they are scheduled through HighLevel's social planner to the accounts you connect.

What it does not fix is relevance. A post nobody cares about is still a post nobody cares about when it goes out on schedule. We keep posts specific to what your business actually does that month, and we never post anything you have not seen.

On AI Empire this becomes full social media management rather than a scheduled batch.

How to judge any AI marketing agency, including this one

Signs of a straight answer

  • Prices you can read before a sales call
  • A clear list of what is set up and who maintains it
  • Plain statements of what they cannot control
  • Review practices that follow the FTC rule and Google's policy
  • A way to hear or see the product working first

Reasons to walk away

  • Promised lead counts, rankings or income
  • Offers to buy, write or filter reviews
  • Cold texting or robocalling lists you never collected
  • AI-written content published without anyone reading it
  • No answer to "what happens if I leave?"
