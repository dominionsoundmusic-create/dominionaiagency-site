An AI chatbot for your website is a chat window, or a talk button, that answers visitors the way you would, using facts you approved, and passes serious inquiries to you with the details already filled in. We set it up, train it on your business and keep it accurate.

Plenty of small business websites already have a chat bubble. Many of them either do nothing useful (a form pretending to be a chat) or say too much (a general AI that will cheerfully invent a price). What we build sits between those: AI chat for a website that is limited to your business, honest about what it does not know, and connected to the CRM where your leads live. It runs on HighLevel's Conversation AI and website chat widget.

Two widgets: one that types, one that talks

The chat widget

The familiar window in the corner of the page. A visitor types a question; the AI replies in a few lines, can ask a follow-up question, and can collect a name, email and phone number when the visitor wants to be contacted or booked. Once we assign a HighLevel Conversation AI bot to the chat widget as one of its channels, and the widget's code is on your site, the AI answers in that window.

The voice widget

What the old version of this site called a talking website. Instead of typing, the visitor taps a button and speaks through the microphone on a phone, tablet or computer, and the AI answers out loud. It uses the same kind of voice agent we put on phone lines. HighLevel still lists the voice widget as a pre-release (beta) feature and says call transfer is not available in it yet, so we point visitors who need a person to your phone number instead.

You can run either one, or both. Many owners start with typed chat because it is quieter and easy to read back later, then add the voice widget if their customers are the kind who would rather pick up the phone than type a paragraph.

Chatbot vs AI: rule-based bots and AI chatbots

The word chatbot covers two different things, and the difference decides what your visitors experience.

Rule-based chatbotAI chatbot (what we set up)
How it worksIf-then logic: buttons, keywords and pre-written repliesA language model reads the question and writes a reply from your knowledge base
What visitors can sayUsually limited to the options shown or simple phrasesOrdinary sentences, typos and odd wording included
PredictabilitySays exactly what was scripted, nothing elseFlexible, so it needs clear instructions and topics it must refuse
Keeping it currentEvery new question means a new branchUpdate the knowledge once and the answers follow
Best forVery narrow jobs, like checking an order statusReal questions about services, hours, booking and next steps

The flexibility of an AI powered chatbot for a website is also its risk. That is why most of our setup time goes into what the bot is told not to do, not into making it sound clever.

What the chatbot learns from

A custom AI chatbot for a website is only custom because of what it is given. HighLevel's knowledge base accepts several kinds of source, and we use whichever fit your business:

  • A written FAQ, in your words, for the questions where the exact answer matters
  • Your website pages, read by a crawler so the bot knows what the site already says
  • Documents you upload, such as a service menu or a policy sheet
  • Short notes on the things you never want said, like prices you only quote in person

When the bot gives an answer, the platform can show which pieces of knowledge it drew on, which is how we track down a wrong answer and fix the source rather than guessing. If your website itself is thin or out of date, that shows up quickly in the chat logs.

Where a conversation goes after the chat

An AI chatbot for customer service is useful only if the conversation ends somewhere. These are the endings we configure.

  1. Answered and done. The visitor got their answer (your hours, whether you serve their area) and left. The transcript is saved so you can see what was asked.
  2. Lead captured. The visitor wants a quote or a call back. The bot collects name, contact details and the job, creates or updates the contact in your CRM, and emails you.
  3. Booked. Where you take appointments, the bot offers open times from your calendar and books one. See AI appointment booking for the rules behind that.
  4. Handed to a person. HighLevel's human handover action pauses the bot and assigns the conversation to you when the visitor asks for a person, the question is outside its knowledge, or it keeps misunderstanding. You pick it up in the same conversation.

From the CRM, follow-up runs by email on the schedule you choose. The CRM automation page covers that part.

Questions it is set up not to answer

The Federal Trade Commission has brought cases against companies that overstated what their AI could do, and visitors are quick to stop trusting a bot that bluffs. So every chatbot we configure has a refusal list. Typical entries:

  • Prices for work that has not been seen or measured
  • Legal, medical, tax or financial questions, beyond pointing to a professional
  • Anything about a specific patient, case or account record
  • Promises about dates, outcomes or availability it cannot check
  • Topics unrelated to your business (it will not write poems for visitors)

When a question lands on that list, the bot says it cannot help with that one and offers a person instead.

A chatbot for small business owners: who it fits

Good fit

  • Your website gets regular visitors who ask the same questions
  • People browse after hours, when the phone is not answered by a person
  • You book appointments or estimates and want requests to arrive complete
  • You already use, or are willing to use, a CRM for leads
